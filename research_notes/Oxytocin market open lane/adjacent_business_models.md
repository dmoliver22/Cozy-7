Method note: ~40 targeted WebSearch queries; all direct page fetches (compoundingfinder.com, bizquest.com, thepeptidecatalog.com) were blocked by the network proxy, so every figure below comes from search-result snippets of the cited page. ## Key Question 1 — GLP-1 wave (2022–2025): who captured value, what affiliates paid, and what happened when compounding ended

### Takeaway
The GLP-1 wave was monetized by small operators mainly through (a) telehealth affiliate/CPA programs paying roughly $4 per lead to ~$25–$57 per paid signup, (b) "cheapest compounded semaglutide" price trackers/directories that route users to telehealth or 503A pharmacies, and (c) micro-telehealth brands fed by affiliates on 10% lifetime revenue share. The shortage-based compounding exemption ended Feb 2025 (compliance deadlines Apr/May 2025), followed by ~100+ FDA warning letters in Sept 2025 and 30 more in Mar 2026 targeting telehealth marketing claims — collapsing the "cheap compounded semaglutide" content angle that trackers were built on.

### Cited Findings

**Affiliate / lead-gen payouts (aggregator-reported unless noted)**
- Hims affiliate program: commission reported variously as 7–10% per sale, "$15–$40+ per new customer sale," "$7–$10 on Partnerize," and "up to 10–30% on online sales"; 30-day cookie; flat-fee CPA on first purchase; VIP rates for volume — [Affilimate](https://affilimate.io/programs/hims-affiliate-program/); [Lasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/hims-loyalty/); [UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/hims/); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/hims-inc-affiliate-program/). (Figures conflict across aggregators; treat the range $7–$40 per new customer as the realistic band, unverified against Hims' own terms.)
- Henry Meds: $25 per subscription purchase, hosted on Impact; PPC/SEM/Google Ads disallowed as promotion mediums; non-converting clicks can get an affiliate removed — [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/henry-meds/); [Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/henry-meds-affiliate-program)
- Found Health: $4 per lead — [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/found-health/)
- Mochi Health: has an affiliate program (job postings for an affiliate-program manager), but no commission figure surfaced — [Marketing Monk job board](https://www.marketingmonk.so/jobboard/jobs/marketing-associate-at-mochi-health-8cebuz)
- Ro: no Ro-specific affiliate payout surfaced in search; results returned only generic Impact.com payout mechanics (payouts on 1st/15th, brand-set minimums) — [Impact guide via JoinOtto](https://joinotto.com/en-gh/influencer-programs/impact)
- Everflow (performance-marketing platform) publishes a GLP-1/semaglutide brands enablement page, indicating GLP-1 telehealth brands run tracked CPA/affiliate programs at scale — [Everflow](https://www.everflow.io/enablement/semaglutide-glp-1-brands-and-everflow)

**Paid acquisition cost benchmarks (agency case study)**
- A Google Ads case study for a semaglutide-injection clinic reported cost per lead of $81 overall, best campaign $50.23/lead with 7% conversion, falling from $83.91 (month 1) to ~$71.54 (month 3) — [Sagapixel case study](https://sagapixel.com/case-study-results/81-cost-per-lead-for-semaglutide/)
- Interpretation: if a telehealth brand's own CPL is $50–$85, a $25 CPA to affiliates (Henry Meds) is cheap for the brand and sets a ceiling on what independent sites can expect per conversion.

**Independent sites / trackers that captured value**
- CompoundingFinder.com runs "cheapest semaglutide" and per-medication pages offering free price-comparison quotes from licensed compounding pharmacies (1–2 business-day turnaround) — a lead-gen/quote-routing model — [CompoundingFinder semaglutide](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/medications/semaglutide); [CompoundingFinder cheapest semaglutide](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/semaglutide)

- PeptideProtocolWiki and HealingMaps also publish 2026 compounded-semaglutide cost guides showing low-end $179–$199/mo, mid $200–$279, high $280–$399 — [PeptideProtocolWiki](https://www.peptideprotocolwiki.com/blog/compounded-semaglutide-cost-guide-2026); [HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/compounded-semaglutide-vs-wegovy-ozempic/)
- WeightLossRankings.org is a "reader-supported" affiliate review site ranking GLP-1 telehealth providers (e.g., Henry Meds review page) and TRT clinics — [WeightLossRankings Henry Meds](https://www.weightlossrankings.org/reviews/henry-meds); [WeightLossRankings TRT](https://www.weightlossrankings.org/best/trt-clinics)

**Micro-telehealth brands built by solo/indie operators (broker/indie disclosures)**
- BizQuest listing (primary broker disclosure): fully remote cash-pay GLP-1/longevity telehealth platform founded 2022; TTM revenue $1,821,000 (+36% YoY), 66% gross margin, $633,000 SDE; acquires patients entirely through social-media affiliates paid a 10% lifetime revenue share, never used paid ads; in-house NPs licensed in 50 states; partner 503A pharmacies drop-ship 3–6 month plans; asking price not shown in snippet — [BizQuest BW2538024](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/nationwide-cash-pay-glp-1-telehealth-platform/BW2538024/)

**What happened when compounding ended**
- FDA declared the semaglutide shortage resolved in Feb 2025; 503A pharmacies had until April and 503B outsourcers until May 2025 to stop — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/fda-declares-semaglutide-shortage-over-spelling-end-compounded-glp-1-market); [Pharmaceutical Technology](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/semaglutide-shortage-ends-as-fda-sets-deadline-for-compounders/); [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/fda-announces-end-semaglutide-shortage-impacting-compounders-and-telehealth)
- Compounded subscriptions were <$200/mo vs >$1,000/mo branded, which was the entire value proposition of the tracker sites — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/well/article/novo-nordisk-wegovy-ozempic-semaglutide-glp-1-shortage-declared-over)
- Some compounders/DTC platforms kept producing after Feb 2025, drawing FDA scrutiny and Novo Nordisk litigation — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/deep-dive/how-glp-1s-broke-healthcare-distribution)
- Branded pivot: Hims & Hers, LifeMD and Ro began offering Wegovy at $499/mo via NovoCare (May 2025) — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/novo-nordisk-expands-glp-1-access-through-telehealth-providers)
- Sept 9, 2025: FDA/HHS sent ~100 warning letters to telehealth and compounding marketers (including Hims & Hers) over "generic"/"clinically proven"/"same active ingredient" claims; 38 state AGs joined in amplifying enforcement — [Safe Medicines](https://www.safemedicines.org/2025/09/sept-15-2025.html); [Mondaq/Birmingham Medical News](https://www.birminghammedicalnews.com/article/9321/fda-hhs-taking-action-against-telehealths-compounded-drug-advertising)
- Mar 3, 2026: 30 more FDA warning letters to telehealth firms; sites were reviewed in Dec 2025; 15 days to respond or face seizure/injunction — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102mmr0/glp1-compliance-fda-targets-telehealth-marketing-in-30-new-warning-letters/)
- As of 2026, CompoundingFinder's own semaglutide page states semaglutide is no longer eligible for compounding — the tracker pivoted to other compounded meds (it now has oxytocin pages; see Q7) — [CompoundingFinder 2026 cost post](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/blog/semaglutide-cost-2026)

### Inferences
- The durable survivors were multi-compound directories (CompoundingFinder, FormBlends, HealingMaps) that could swap the headline molecule when semaglutide compounding closed; single-molecule "cheapest semaglutide" sites lost their core keyword in a single regulatory event.
- The affiliate-fed micro-telehealth model (10% lifetime rev share to creators) is the one structure where small operators captured seven-figure value — but it requires a licensed prescriber network and pharmacy partnerships, not just a website.
- Hype-cycle entry: value accrued to sites launched 2022–early 2024 (shortage window). Entrants after Feb 2025 inherited the enforcement phase.

### Gaps
- Ro's affiliate commission could not be found; Mochi's commission could not be found.
- No disclosed revenue for any pure GLP-1 comparison/tracker site (CompoundingFinder, FormBlends) surfaced; their monetization (quote fees vs. affiliate) is inferred from page design, not disclosed.
- No Flippa/Empire Flippers *completed sale* of a GLP-1 content site with price and multiple surfaced; only broker asking prices for telehealth operators.

## Key Question 2 — Peptide wave (2024–2026): how info/vendor-comparison sites and creators monetize; commission rates; ad-policy and FDA constraints

### Takeaway
Peptide monetization is affiliate-heavy with unusually high commissions: research-chemical vendors pay 10–25% (some wellness-brand programs advertise 70–80% on first orders), and peptide telehealth clinics pay 12–20% first-order plus 5–12% recurring. Top creators (Jay Campbell, Hunter Williams) went vertical — from affiliate codes to co-founding their own vendor (BioLongevity Labs). Google Ads does not ban peptides outright but "most do not survive the first 90 days" of enforcement; FDA placed BPC-157 on the 503A Category 2 list (late 2023), then reversed course in Apr–Jul 2026 with a PCAC vote to recommend adding it to the bulks list.

### Cited Findings

**Vendor affiliate commissions (aggregator- or program-page-reported)**
- Better Molecule: 80% commission on new-customer sales — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/better-molecule-affiliate-program/)
- Prime Science Peptides: 70% on new customers, 5% on returning — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/prime-science-peptides-affiliate-program/)
- Peptide Hackers: 15% of net sales; CertaPeptides/Evolve Peptides: 15–25% — [stonevillenc.org roundup](https://stonevillenc.org/peptide-affiliate-research/) (low-authority aggregator; unverified)
- Kindr Peptides: 12% recurring on subscriptions for customer lifetime, 8% on one-time orders, 5% + $250 bonus on practitioner/clinic wholesale accounts with first order ≥$2,500 — [Kindr affiliate page](https://city-kindr-engine.lovable.app/affiliates/peptides)
- Ascend Peptides UK: 10% base, up to 12.5–15% on performance (Partnero); Ascension Peptides: 10% — [Partnero](https://list.partnero.com/programs/515); [revshare.so](https://www.revshare.so/programs/advertiser/ascension-peptides)

**Peptide telehealth clinic affiliate programs**
- Argo (argorx): 20% on first order, 10% recurring for 12 months, 60-day cookie, Net-30 monthly payouts — [Argo affiliates](https://argorx.lovable.app/affiliates)
- Peptide Therapy Rx: recurring commissions, 90-day cookie; PeptideStack: affiliate program exists, rate not shown — [PeptideStack](https://peptidestack.io/affiliate)
- HealingMaps publishes "The 4 Best Peptide Telehealth Programs of 2026" (directory-style comparison with the compounded-peptide angle) — [HealingMaps peptide telehealth](https://healingmaps.com/peptide-telehealth/)
- MyPeptideMatch.com runs "best peptides for tissue repair/recovery" comparison pages — [MyPeptideMatch](https://www.mypeptidematch.com/best/tissue-repair-recovery)

**Creator playbook: affiliate code → own vendor**
- Jay Campbell used a "Jay15" 15%-off affiliate code with Limitless Life Nootropics and is listed as an influencer backing Limitless — [Outliyr](https://outliyr.com/?p=46300)
- Campbell and Hunter Williams (Hunter's Health Hacks, Beehiiv) co-founded BioLongevity Labs with Josh Felber; Williams wrote that after "years" of free/premium peptide education and promoting vendors, they launched their own vendor stocking "every peptide, bioregulator, small molecule," GMP US manufacturing, credit-card processing, crypto coming — [Hunter's Health Hacks](https://huntershealthhacks.beehiiv.com/p/launching-a-research-peptide-company); [Pulse 2.0 interview](https://pulse2.com/biolongevity-labs-profile-jay-campbell-interview/)
- Paid community pricing: "Exploring Peptides" on Skool charges $15/month (classroom videos, research breakdowns, monthly live Q&A, bundled app access valued at $20/mo) — [Skool Exploring Peptides](https://www.skool.com/exploring-peptides)

**Ad-policy constraints**
- Google Ads 2026 guide: peptide brands "can run Google Ads" but "most do not survive the first 90 days without getting flagged or suspended"; the category is "not banned but heavily enforced" at the intersection of healthcare/medicines, unapproved substances, and dangerous products policies — [ABNewswire via FinancialContent](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/abnewswire-2026-9-10-google-ads-policy-for-peptide-brands-the-complete-2026-guide)
- Google's Unapproved Substances policy blocks ads for prescription drugs without authorization and supplements making health claims — [StubGroup glossary](https://stubgroup.com/glossary/unapproved-substances-policy/)
- Google banned pill presses/encapsulating machines from Sept 1, 2025 — [Google Ads policy update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16328882?hl=en-GB)
- Fiverr gigs exist specifically for "compliant Google Ads for peptide and supplement brands without suspension," signaling how routine suspension is — [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/leadconvertingz/run-compliant-google-ads-for-peptide-and-supplement-brands-without-suspension)
- Henry Meds (GLP-1) explicitly disallows PPC/SEM/Google Ads for affiliates (see Q1), a pattern likely common across telehealth programs.

**FDA actions on peptides**
- Late 2023: FDA placed BPC-157 on the 503A Category 2 bulk list ("significant safety concerns": immunogenicity, no human data); selling bulk BPC-157 for human use = adulterated/misbranded unapproved drug — [DJ Holt Law](https://djholtlaw.com/regulatory-alert-the-legal-status-of-bpc-157-in-compounding-and-clinical-practice/)
- Apr 2026: HHS/FDA pulled 12 peptides (incl. BPC-157) out of Category 2 and sent them to PCAC for review — [Project Biohacking](https://projectbiohacking.beehiiv.com/p/bpc-157-off-list-not-legal-2026)
- Jul 23, 2026: PCAC voted to recommend adding BPC-157 to the 503A bulks list despite FDA reviewers' evidence concerns; proposed rule + comment period still required; earliest legal compounding window estimated mid–late 2027 — [AmSpa](https://www.americanmedspa.org/?p=21030); [The Peptide Catalog](https://thepeptidecatalog.com/articles/peptide-telehealth-market-fda-vote)
- Telehealth-marketing podcast framed the vote as "Peptides Just Got a Green Light" for telehealth marketing — [CastFox / Brand Building Podcast](https://www.castfox.net/podcast/the-brand-building-podcast-6302704/episode/peptides-just-got-a-green-light-heres-what-it-means-for-telehealth-marketing-503043)

### Inferences
- The 70–80% first-order commissions are almost certainly loss-leader CPA offers from supplement-style "peptide" brands with high LTV subscriptions, not research-chemical vendors; the realistic band for a content site is 10–25%.
- Because paid search is effectively closed to peptide advertisers, organic/SEO content sites and newsletters are structurally advantaged — vendors must buy traffic through affiliates, which is why commissions are high.
- The regulatory arc (ban → reconsideration → advisory vote) created three distinct content windows: "is it legal?" (2024–25), "RFK review" (Apr–Jul 2026), "when can I get it compounded?" (now). Sites that published across all three captured compounding-interest traffic.

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue or traffic for any peptide comparison site (PepFinder-type) surfaced.
- Peptide Sciences' affiliate terms specifically did not surface.
- No documented FTC or FDA action *against an affiliate/content site* (as opposed to vendors/telehealth) surfaced in search.
- Meta ad policy for peptides specifically not found.

## Key Question 3 — Ketamine therapy: how directories earn; what happened to Psychable

### Takeaway
Ketamine/psychedelic directories monetize via tiered paid listings ($49–$99/mo per provider at Third Wave), featured placements, and referral/affiliate fees (Mindbloom pays ~$57.60/sale or 40% via Impact); the most developed operator (HealingMaps, ~1,800 clinics) raised $1M seed and layered on a clinic GPO in 2025. I found no evidence Psychable shut down — it still appears as active on CB Insights/LinkedIn, though its site now serves from a Render subdomain (psychableai.onrender.com), which is suggestive of a wind-down or re-platforming but unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Third Wave directory pricing (primary pricing page): Verified listing free after ID review; Vetted listing $49/mo or $399/yr for therapists/coaches, $99/mo or $799/yr for retreats/clinics — [Third Wave Get Listed](https://directory.thethirdwave.co/get-listed/)
- Mindbloom affiliate: $57.60 per sale via FlexOffers; 40% per sale via Impact.com; 30-day cookie; targeted at wellness influencers/mental-health creators — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/mindbloom-affiliate-program/); [LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/mindbloom/)
- HealingMaps: founded 2020 (Miami) by Cory Jones (ex-Playboy CCO), Mark Goldhar, Raymond Harari; $1M seed 2022; revenue model "featured listings … advertising or referral fees"; 2025 launched Healing Health Alliance, a GPO for ketamine/psychedelic/TMS clinics — [StartupIntros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/healingmaps); [HealingMaps GPO press release](https://healingmaps.com/healingmaps-and-advanced-behavioral-strategies-launch-healing-health-alliance-the-first-group-purchasing-organization-gpo-exclusively-for-ketamine-psychedelic-medicine-and-tms-clinics/)
- HealingMaps tracks ~1,800 verified ketamine clinics (May 2026), +60% vs 2022; only ~1 in 10 listings post a price; national median infusion $400 (IV $450, IM $350, KAP $650, Spravato cash $750); telehealth ketamine $250/session or $399–$599/mo — [HealingMaps 2026 cost analysis](https://healingmaps.com/ketamine-infusion-cost-2026-directory-analysis/); [HealingMaps Oct 2025 roundup](https://healingmaps.com/october-2025-roundup-what-ketamine-treatment-costs-across-the-united-states/)
- HealingMaps' SEO footprint: city pages ("13 Best Ketamine Clinics in Phoenix (2026) — Verified & Reviewed"), monthly cost roundups, "near me" pages — [HealingMaps Phoenix](https://healingmaps.com/the-best-ketamine-clinics-in-phoenix/)
- Psychedelic.Support (launched Mar 2018): curated listing of licensed providers (must be licensed or have trial experience, agree to Code of Conduct); revenue from accredited CE, online courses, and the directory; listing price not found — [Chacruna](https://chacruna.net/psychedelic-support-innovation-technology/); [Psymposia](https://ghost.psymposia.com/psychedelic-support-creates-a-network-for-psychedelic-therapists/)
- Psychable: launched Apr 2021 (LA), directory + practitioner reviews + social network; CB Insights/LinkedIn (543 followers) list it as active; its pages now resolve at psychableai.onrender.com — [Psychable launch press](https://psychableai.onrender.com/press/psychable-launch-psychedelic-therapy-community); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/psychable)
- No source stating Psychable was shut down, acquired or defunct was found.

### Inferences
- Directory economics: at Third Wave's rates, 100 paying clinic listings ≈ $80–$120K/yr; a solo operator with 30–50 paying providers would clear $20–$60K/yr before any affiliate revenue — the only directory model with publicly posted unit pricing.
- HealingMaps' path (directory → price-transparency data → GPO) shows the upsell route: once you aggregate clinics, you can sell to clinics (purchasing, software) rather than to patients.
- Psychable's move from a .com to a free Render subdomain and "psychableai" branding is consistent with a near-zero-cost maintenance mode after the 2021–22 psychedelic funding hype faded; this is an inference, not a documented fact.

### Gaps
- Ketamine Clinics Directory (ketamineclinicsdirectory.com) listing fees not found.
- No disclosed revenue for HealingMaps, Psychable, Third Wave directory, or Psychedelic.Support.
- Psychable's actual status (dormant vs. operating) could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 5 — Niche health newsletters and paid communities (Substack/Beehiiv/Skool): counts, pricing, sponsorship rates

### Takeaway
Disclosed niche-specific numbers are scarce; what is public is platform-level: Substack newsletters typically charge $5–$20/mo (Substack takes 10% + Stripe fees), Beehiiv sponsorships run ~$200–$1,000 per placement at 5–10K subs and $500–$5K at 10–50K, and the one peptide community with posted pricing (Skool "Exploring Peptides") charges $15/mo. Peptide newsletters (Hunter's Health Hacks, Project Biohacking) on Beehiiv monetize primarily through vendor affiliate codes and, in Hunter Williams' case, by launching a vendor.

## Key Question 6 — Which models require licensure or no-health-claims discipline; which have been hit by FTC health-claim enforcement or state telehealth advertising rules

### Takeaway
Pure information/directory/affiliate sites need no license but are bound by FTC endorsement/health-claims rules and increasingly by platform policy (Google's healthcare and unapproved-substances policies; affiliate programs banning PPC). Enforcement to date has landed on the *advertisers* — telehealth brands (FTC v. NextMed, Jul 2025; FDA's ~130 warning letters Sept 2025–Mar 2026) and compounders — not on independent affiliates, but the flagged claim types ("same active ingredient as X," "clinically proven," "generic Ozempic," hidden pricing) are exactly the copy affiliates reproduce.

### Cited Findings
- FTC settled with Southern Health Solutions/NextMed (Jul 14, 2025) over false pricing ("only $79 for your first month" without disclosing medication/lab/membership/early-termination costs), misleading weight-loss claims, and fake testimonials — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/08/health-care-marketing-enforcement-ftc-targets-telehealth-company-for-deceptive-w/); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ftcs-nextmed-settlement-signals-enforcement-against-telemedicine-companies-making-false-or-misleading-drug-claims/)
- FTC's health-marketing priority rests on its 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance; scope includes OTC drugs, homeopathics, devices, diagnostics, health apps; FTC has also acted against telehealth platforms for sharing health data with ad platforms — [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/03/ftc-developments-impacting-healthcare-enforcement)
- FDA Sept 2025 (~100 letters) and Mar 2026 (30 letters) targeted telehealth websites' claims; flagged examples: "Weekly injectable GLP-1 with the same active ingredient as Ozempic and Wegovy," "Clinically proven ingredients," "SEMAGLUTIDE (GENERIC OZEMPIC/WEGOVY)" — [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/blog/fda-glp-1-compounding-crackdown-what-telehealth-clinicians-need-to-know/); [BenefitsPro](https://www.benefitspro.com/amp/2026/03/05/fda-issues-warning-letters-to-30-telehealth-firms-over-glp-1-marketing/)
- Hims & Hers' Feb 2025 Super Bowl ad for compounded GLP-1 drew a Durbin–Marshall letter to FDA (no risk disclosure) and a new legal-liability risk factor in its 10-K — [Safe Medicines](https://www.safemedicines.org/2025/02/hims-and-hers-ad-liability.html); [Managed Healthcare Executive](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/hims-and-hers-superbowl-ad-draws-controversy-over-safety)
- State AGs: a 38-AG coalition amplified federal enforcement on compounded GLP-1 marketing; AGs also urged Meta to restrict compounded weight-loss drug ads (no formal Meta ban found) — [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1685456/fda-hhs-taking-action-against-telehealths-compounded-drug-advertising); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-faces-scrutiny-over-misleading-weight-loss-drug-ads)
- Private litigation: telehealth companies sued over compounded GLP-1 sales and corporate-practice-of-medicine violations — [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/print/v2/content/49047879/Telehealth-Companies-Sued-for-Selling-Compounded-GLP-1-Drugs-and-Violations-of-Corporate-Practice-of-Medicine-Laws.pdf)
- Legislative: SAFE Drugs Act of 2025 would expand FDA oversight of compounded GLP-1s and telehealth providers — [Dykema](https://dykema.com/news-insights/congress-introduces-safe-drugs-act-of-2025-expanding-fda-oversight-of-compounded-glp-1-drugs-and-telehealth-providers.html)
- State pharmacy boards: Ohio limited compounders to 250 units per batch of a drug incl. sterile injectables — [Safe Medicines Sept 2025](https://www.safemedicines.org/2025/09/sept-15-2025.html)
- Platform-level: affiliate programs ban PPC/trademark bidding (Henry Meds, Alloy); Google heavily enforces peptide ads (Q2); Partnership for Safe Medicines actively files complaints against telehealth ads — [Cuelinks Henry Meds](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/henry-meds-affiliate-program); [affi.io Alloy](https://affi.io/m/alloy)

**Licensure map (synthesized from the above)**
- No license needed: directory, price tracker, comparison/review site, newsletter, paid community, affiliate links, sponsorships.
- License/clinical partners needed: micro-telehealth brand (NPs/MDs in 50 states + 503A pharmacy partners, per BizQuest listing in Q1); any site that itself "prescribes," "dispenses," or sells peptides/oxytocin "for human use."
- Grey zone: research-peptide vendors (sold "not for human consumption"); creator-owned vendors (BioLongevity Labs model).

### Inferences
- An unlicensed oxytocin content operator's legal exposure is primarily (1) FTC endorsement/affiliate-disclosure rules, (2) FTC substantiation if the site itself makes efficacy claims, (3) platform policy. Letting the *telehealth partner's* copy make the claim and keeping the site descriptive/comparative is the pattern of the surviving directories.
- No small affiliate/directory has been named in an FDA/FTC action in this period per surfaced sources; the enforcement risk is indirect (partner programs get shut, keywords disappear).

### Gaps
- No specific state telehealth *advertising* statute (as opposed to compounding/CPOM rules) targeting content sites surfaced.
- No documented FTC action against a health affiliate site in 2022–2026 surfaced; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## Key Question 7 — Mapping to oxytocin: which 2–3 models fit, and 12-month solo-operator revenue ranges

### Takeaway
Oxytocin today looks like peptides in 2024, not GLP-1 in 2023: compounded intranasal spray is legally available by off-label prescription from 503A pharmacies at $21–$120 per fill, several telehealth routes exist, there is no FDA-approved intranasal product, efficacy evidence is thin, and biotech (Tonix TNX-1900) keeps the news flow alive. The affiliate economics are poor (cheap molecule → small carts), so the fit is (1) a multi-compound-ready comparison/directory with telehealth CPA, (2) a newsletter/paid community riding the biotech + "love hormone" narrative, and (3) a practitioner-side listing/B2B play; a micro-telehealth brand is the high-ceiling option but requires licensed partners.

### Cited Findings (oxytocin-specific)
- No FDA-approved intranasal or sublingual oxytocin exists in the US; prescribers must write to a compounding pharmacy; must be dispensed under valid prescription — [Superpower oxytocin guide](https://superpower.com/guides/peptides/oxytocin); [Empower Pharmacy](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray)

- CompoundingFinder (Sept 2026): across 8 compounding pharmacies, oxytocin nasal sprays run $1.67–$16.50 per bottle(-equivalent unit as stated in snippet), typical fill $21–$120; the site already has "cheapest oxytocin" and "cheapest oxytocin nasal spray" pages — [CompoundingFinder oxytocin guide](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/blog/compounded-oxytocin-cost-guide); [CompoundingFinder cheapest oxytocin](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin-nasal-spray)

### Model fit and 12-month revenue ranges (estimates, derived from Q1–Q6 benchmarks; NOT disclosed figures)

**Model A — Comparison/price-tracker + telehealth CPA (CompoundingFinder/FormBlends pattern, oxytocin-first but multi-compound-ready)**
- Mechanics: "cheapest compounded oxytocin spray" tracker, pharmacy/telehealth comparison, quote routing; monetize via telehealth CPA ($10–$57 per paid signup per Alloy/Henry/Mindbloom benchmarks) and peptide-clinic affiliate (12–20% first order, 5–12% recurring per Kindr/Argo).
- Constraint: oxytocin fills are $21–$120, so percentage commissions yield $2–$20 per order; need flat CPA deals with telehealth partners or bundling with adjacent higher-ticket compounds (peptides, TRT, NAD+).
- Hype-stage: early/ascending (no mainstream enforcement yet; biotech news flow; low competition — CompoundingFinder is the only visible incumbent with oxytocin pages).
- 12-month solo range (estimate): $3K–$30K; upside toward $50K+ only if a telehealth partner signs a flat $25+ CPA and the site ranks for "oxytocin nasal spray prescription/online" terms. Unverified.

**Model B — Newsletter + paid community (Hunter's Health Hacks / Exploring Peptides pattern)**
- Mechanics: free Beehiiv/Substack newsletter on oxytocin science, biotech trials (Tonix, autism/social-anxiety research), protocols, pharmacy pricing; paid tier $8–$15/mo (Skool peptide community benchmark $15); sponsorships from compounding pharmacies, peptide vendors, telehealth clinics at $200–$1,000/placement once 5–10K subs.
- Hype-stage fit: strongest cultural hook of any compound in this set ("love hormone," bonding, autism, sexual function), suited to narrative content; thin efficacy evidence means claims discipline is critical (FTC substantiation).
- 12-month solo range (estimate): $2K–$25K (e.g., 150–300 paid subs at $10 ≈ $16–$32K gross before 10% platform + Stripe; plus 0–2 sponsors/month). Unverified.

**Model C — Practitioner directory / B2B listing (Third Wave / HealingMaps pattern)**
- Mechanics: directory of prescribers, compounding pharmacies and clinics offering oxytocin (and adjacent peptide/HRT services); $49–$99/mo "vetted" listings per Third Wave pricing; later upsell (GPO, software, lead routing) per HealingMaps.
- Constraint: supply side is thin today (few prescribers market oxytocin), so launch as a peptide/compounded-hormone directory with an oxytocin vertical.
- 12-month solo range (estimate): $0–$15K in year one (10–25 paying listings at $49–$99/mo), with directory value compounding if the compound mainstreams. Unverified.

**Model D (high-ceiling, requires partners) — Affiliate-fed micro-telehealth brand (BizQuest pattern)**
- $1.8M revenue / $633K SDE example in GLP-1 used 10% lifetime affiliate rev share, 50-state NP network, 503A drop-ship; for oxytocin the cart is far smaller ($21–$120/fill) so a comparable business would need subscription bundling with other compounds. Not a solo-operator model without licensed partners; flagged for completeness.

### Inferences
- Oxytocin's weak unit economics (cheap generic, small fills) mean percentage-based affiliate models under-deliver; the GLP-1 and peptide winners relied on $100–$500/month subscriptions. Flat CPA with telehealth partners, or audience monetization (community/sponsorship), fits better than vendor rev-share.
- The single biggest transferable lesson from GLP-1 is to build multi-compound from day one: CompoundingFinder survived the semaglutide shutdown by having pages for every compounded drug, including oxytocin, while single-molecule trackers lost their keyword overnight.
- Regulatory tailwind/risk: oxytocin is an old, approved-for-labor generic, so (unlike BPC-157) it isn't on a Category 2 list and compounding of an approved drug's alternate dosage form is routine; the enforcement risk is marketing claims (efficacy for autism/social anxiety/libido), which is FTC territory, not an FDA compounding ban.
