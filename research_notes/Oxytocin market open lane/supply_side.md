# Supply Side of the US Consumer/Clinical Oxytocin Market (October 2026)

> **Method and reliability note — read first.** Direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by network policy for this task, so **every finding below is drawn from web-search result snippets, not from reading the source pages.** Separately, the turn's shared web-search budget was exhausted after ~31 queries, so two planned follow-up searches (characterizing `mypeptidematch.com` and `medfinder.com`) could not be run; they are logged as gaps.
>
> **A second, more important caveat:** a large share of the sites that supply the pricing and "directory" claims below show the hallmarks of **programmatic / AI-generated SEO content rather than established commercial operations**: `lovable.app` subdomains (a vibe-coding host), exhaustive per-state and per-city URL permutations (e.g. `compoundingfinder.com/medications/oxytocin/idaho/twin-falls`), self-asserted "as of April 2026" verification counts, and near-identical boilerplate across competing sites. This is itself a substantive finding about the market (see Q4), but it means **the dollar figures below should be treated as claimed, snippet-level, and largely unverified** — not as surveyed market prices. I have marked confidence per item.

## Q1: Which clinics or therapists actually use oxytocin in couples/marriage counseling, sex therapy, or psychotherapy?

### Takeaway
Almost none, as a named and findable practice. The canonical example — the Glendale, AZ "Wellness Solutions" clinic — is real but is a **circa-2013 news story, not a current offering**, and I found no evidence it still operates this way. The live clinical supply is not psychotherapy-adjacent at all; it sits in **peptide/hormone/functional-medicine and ketamine clinics**, where oxytocin is one SKU on a 10–25 compound menu, sold as "intimacy/bonding," with no therapy attached.

### Cited Findings
- **Wellness Solutions, Glendale AZ (historical, ~2013).** Matt French, owner of the Wellness Solutions Clinic in Glendale, Arizona, "started treating people in marriage counseling with a daily dose of oxytocin in hopes of promoting love and trust between couples"; couples took "a bio-identical form of the hormone in the form of a dissolvable oral strip while working to resolve their issues in therapy." French: "If a couple is struggling with bonding issues, with intimacy issues, it could be as a result of inadequate levels of the hormone oxytocin." — [TIME Healthland, "Should the 'Love Hormone' Oxytocin Be Used in Couples Therapy?"](https://healthland.time.com/?p=25314) *(Note: TIME Healthland ceased publishing in the mid-2010s; this is a historical artifact, and the snippet's "began in September" is undated relative to today.)*
- The same TIME piece carries contemporaneous expert pushback: a researcher "expressed concern about its safety and skepticism regarding its effectiveness," and notes "several studies finding a link between high levels of oxytocin and relationship problems in women." — [TIME Healthland](https://healthland.time.com/?p=25314)
- **Academic couples work is research, not commerce.** A UNSW study "tested whether oxytocin could help relationships by boosting empathy and communication," with a free trial of "five assessment and counselling sessions by psychologists," delivering oxytocin as a nasal spray. Participants on oxytocin "exhibited significantly more positive behavior, such as listening, confirming or laughing during conflict, compared to those getting placebo." — [UNSW Newsroom](https://newsroom.unsw.edu.au/news/unleashing-love-hormone)
- An RCT exists on intranasal oxytocin as an adjunct to **Alcohol Behavioral Couple Therapy** — i.e. the couples-therapy-plus-oxytocin combination lives in trial protocols. — [The Journal of Clinical Psychiatry (PDF)](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Randomized-Controlled-Trial-Efficacy-Intranasal-Oxytocin-Enhance-Alcohol-Behavioral-Couple-Therapy-24m15627.pdf); see also [NCT02941692 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/92/NCT02941692/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)
- **Ketamine/psychedelic clinics are the real adjacent adopters.** A HealingMaps trade article argues ketamine clinics are "perfectly positioned to add peptide therapy" because they "operate outside the insurance system, attract patients committed to investing in their health, and already deliver complex injectable and infusion therapies," and already have "infusion suites and injection protocols, staff trained in monitoring patients through novel therapies, and the clinical workflow for receiving, storing, and administering compounded pharmaceuticals." — [HealingMaps](https://healingmaps.com/ketamine-clinics-add-peptide-therapy/)
- **Named clinics listing oxytocin on a peptide menu:**
  - *Apollo Health*, Gig Harbor WA — "physician-led practice… offering 11+ peptide compounds including proprietary GLOW and KLOW blends and Oxytocin." — [HealingMaps listing](https://healingmaps.com/listing/apollo-health-gig-harbor-washington/)
  - *The Institute for Integrative Therapies*, Eden Prairie MN — "uniquely combines peptide therapy with ketamine-assisted psychotherapy and psychedelic-informed care." — [HealingMaps listing](https://healingmaps.com/listing/institute-integrative-therapies-eden-prairie-minnesota/)
  - *Dr. Michael Aziz*, New York NY — described as "the most expansive peptide menu in NYC with over 25 compounds." — [HealingMaps listing](https://healingmaps.com/listing/dr-michael-aziz-md-new-york-city-new-york/)
  - *Executive Medicine of Texas*, Southlake TX (Drs. J. Mark Anderson, Walter N. Gaman) — [HealingMaps listing](https://healingmaps.com/listing/executive-medicine-of-texas-southlake-texas/)
  - *Dr. Diane Brzezinski*, Naples FL — "peptide therapy under a board-certified DO." — [HealingMaps listing](https://healingmaps.com/listing/dr-diane-brzezinski-internal-medicine-naples-florida/)
  - Further oxytocin-adjacent/peptide listings surfaced for: Healor (Las Vegas NV), Collectiv Bio Wellness (Colorado Springs CO), Apostol Integrative Functional Medicine (Flower Mound TX), Peak Performance & Prevention P3 Life (Puyallup WA), Forward Healthy Lifestyles (Germantown WI), Atlas Men's Health (NYC), Peptides & Hormones (Fort Lauderdale FL), Treasure Valley Pain & Hormones (Meridian ID), Olympia Aesthetics & Wellness (Palm Harbor FL), Life Enhancement Clinics of Montana (Bozeman), Infinity Life Center (Honolulu HI). — [HealingMaps listings index](https://healingmaps.com/listings/page/14/)
- **Sex-medicine framing exists but is prescriber-led, not therapist-led.** "While oxytocin is not FDA approved for uses of sexual function, some healthcare providers prescribe it off-label and have it compounded as a nasal spray for patients with orgasmic disorder, mood disorder, and/or loss of libido." — [Defy Medical](https://defymedical.com/?p=1369)
- A sexual-medicine practice markets a compounded multi-ingredient "all-in-one aphrodisiac / arousal / intimacy enhancer" — [Berman Sexual Health](https://bermansexualhealth.com/the-secret-all-in-one-aphrodisiac-sexual-arousal-enhancer-and-intimacy-enhancer/)
- Case-report literature underpins the sexual-function positioning: a 2012 *Journal of Sexual Medicine* report on a 32-year-old man taking intranasal oxytocin off-label for social anxiety who saw improvement in "libido, erection, and orgasm"; a 1994 *BMJ* case of a woman reporting increased desire and satisfaction after intranasal oxytocin. — [Defy Medical](https://defymedical.com/?p=1369); [Male Anorgasmia Treated with Oxytocin, J Sex Med](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jsmed/pdf/01222926-200804000-00040~male-anorgasmia-treated-with-oxytocin); [TIME Healthland](https://healthland.time.com/?p=56985)

### Inferences
- The "oxytocin-assisted couples therapy" category that the Glendale story implies **essentially does not exist as a commercial offering in 2026**. The supply migrated to a different frame: a peptide product sold to an individual consumer for "intimacy," decoupled from any relational therapy.
- Where oxytocin does appear in a clinic, it is a **menu-filler, not a specialty**. No clinic in these results positions itself *as* an oxytocin or bonding clinic. That means there is no supplier set with a strong enough identity to self-organize into a category — a structural precondition for a directory being valuable.
- The licensing split is the likely reason: psychotherapists and most sex therapists (LMFT/LCSW/PhD) **cannot prescribe**, and prescribers are not trained in couples work. Nothing in these results shows anyone bridging that gap commercially — which is arguably the single clearest "missing connective layer" the research surfaced.

### Gaps
- **How many such clinics exist, countable.** HealingMaps' own figure is "487 verified peptide clinics as of April 2026" ([listing pages](https://healingmaps.com/listing/healor-las-vegas-nevada/)), but that is peptide clinics generally, self-asserted, and I could not verify how many carry oxytocin specifically. No independent census found.
- **Clinic-level oxytocin pricing.** No clinic published an oxytocin-specific price in any snippet. Defy Medical's pricing is explicitly not public.
- **Whether Wellness Solutions (Glendale) still offers this.** Could not verify; no current page surfaced. The practice may be defunct or rebranded. A Theravive ZIP-based marriage-counseling listing for 85303 appeared ([Theravive](https://theravive.com/zip/az/marriage-counseling-85303.aspx)) but with no oxytocin connection.
- **AASECT / professional-body position on oxytocin.** Searched directly; no AASECT guidance surfaced. Unknown.
- I did not find a single named **therapist** (as opposed to prescriber) advertising oxytocin-adjacent practice.

### Inferences
- **The monthly price band is tight and high relative to drug cost.** $115–$206/mo retail against a claimed pharmacy fill of ~$21–$120 (Q3) implies the telehealth margin is in the *prescribing and subscription wrapper*, not the molecule. That is the economic signature of a market where the scarce input is **prescriber willingness**, not supply.
- **Framing has consolidated on "intimacy/connection," not "mood/social anxiety."** FormBlends' "thirty to sixty minutes before shared time" and Superpower's "pair-bond" copy are couples/sex-life positioning. Yet the *clinical* literature base (Q1) is social cognition, anxiety and PTSD. There is a **marketing-to-evidence mismatch** worth flagging to the report writer.
- **No oxytocin pure-play telehealth brand was found.** Every verified seller is a GLP-1, TRT, longevity or multi-drug platform. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the category is **not yet consolidated** — and therefore that a connective/comparison layer has nothing branded to connect.
- The **bundled troche** (oxytocin + bremelanotide + tadalafil) is a notable product-design move: it converts a weak-evidence peptide into a sexual-performance product with two ingredients that *do* have strong effects, which makes head-to-head comparison across sellers much harder for a consumer.

### Gaps
- No verified price for Push Health, Defy Medical, Outliyr, or the hitdirector portal.
- Superpower's "$89/month" came through a search-result summary rather than a quoted product page; **treat as low confidence.**
- One snippet (via medfinder.com) claimed oxytocin telehealth "programs start around $25/month," which is **wildly inconsistent** with every other figure found and with pharmacy cost. I regard it as unreliable. — [MedFinder](https://www.medfinder.com/blog/category/drug-guides?page=209)
- Could not determine which of these companies own vs. contract their pharmacy, nor any volume/revenue figures for any of them.

## Q3: Compounding pharmacies (503A/503B) — forms, strengths, and price comparison

### Takeaway
Oxytocin nasal spray is a **503A (patient-specific) product only** — notably Empower, a large compounder with both 503A and 503B facilities, makes it **only through its 503A arm**, which means no office-stock/bulk channel and therefore no commodity price discovery. A price-comparison layer **does exist** (`compoundingfinder.com`), but it is a **quote-request lead-gen form, not a live price engine**, and its numbers are internally inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- **Standard form and strengths:** "Compounded oxytocin nasal spray is typically dispensed in **10 mL multidose bottles at concentrations of 50 IU/mL or 100 IU/mL.**" — [Empower Pharmacy](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray)
- **Typical regimen as published by compounders:** "one 0.1 mL metered spray per nostril (approximately 10 IU total) administered 30–45 minutes before the desired behavioral effect, up to twice daily; clinicians may adjust by 5–10 IU increments." — [Empower Pharmacy](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray)
- **503A-only channel:** Empower "operates licensed 503A and 503B facilities," but its oxytocin nasal spray "is available solely through their **503A Compounding Pharmacy, not their 503B facility**. A valid prescription is required." — [Empower Pharmacy](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray)
- **No FDA-approved intranasal product exists:** "there is no FDA-approved intranasal oxytocin product in the United States, so all nasal spray formulations must be compounded by a pharmacy with a valid prescription." FDA-approved oxytocin (e.g. injectable Pitocin) is "only approved for use in labor and postpartum care"; intranasal use "for mood, anxiety, sleep, social bonding, pain, or any other indication" is off-label. — [CompoundingFinder](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/blog/compounded-oxytocin-cost-guide); [medx.it.com](https://medx.it.com/how-can-i-get-prescribed-oxytocin-a-guide-to-therapeutic-uses-and-pathways) *(medx.it.com appears to be an SEO content site — low confidence on its own)*
- **Other named compounders:**
  - *Empower Pharmacy* (TX) — 503A + 503B; [site](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray), [MTEC profile](https://mtec-sc.org/life-sciences/empower-pharmacy)
  - *CareFirst Specialty Pharmacy (CFS Pharmacy)* — "a nationally accredited **PCAB** compounding pharmacy that compounds oxytocin and offers fax and electronic prescription services," phone (800) 786-1405 / (844) 922-7379 — [CFS Pharmacy](https://cfspharmacy.pharmacy/oxytocin-compounded)
  - *PCI Pharmacy* — maintains an intake form specifically titled "Oxytocin Nasal Spray" — [PCI jotform](https://pci.jotform.com/form/243086705078057)
- **Claimed pharmacy price ranges (CompoundingFinder's own data):**
  - Oxytocin nasal spray: **"$21 to $120 per fill," median ~$60/bottle** — [CompoundingFinder nasal spray](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin-nasal-spray)
  - Oxytocin troches: **"$3 to $6.67 per troche or $10 to $200 per fill"** — [CompoundingFinder troches](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin-troches)
  - All oxytocin forms: **"$10 to $355.50"**; "package quotes commonly run $21 to $150 with a median near $75; per-unit quotes commonly run $3 to $12" — [CompoundingFinder oxytocin](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin)
  - A separate snippet claims "around **$200 per 90-day supply** at some pharmacies" — [CompoundingFinder cost guide](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/blog/compounded-oxytocin-cost-guide)
- **What CompoundingFinder actually is:** "allows you to **compare oxytocin price ranges** from compounding pharmacies **before requesting a personalized pharmacy quote.**" Mechanism: "submit your medication details including your ZIP code" → "**Within 1–2 business days, receive competing quotes from multiple pharmacies.**" "The service is **free to use and requires no account**." "You will still need a valid prescription before a pharmacy can dispense." — [CompoundingFinder oxytocin](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin); [find-a-provider search result](https://cfspharmacy.pharmacy/oxytocin-compounded)
- **Its coverage is a state/city URL lattice**, e.g. `/cheapest/oxytocin/illinois`, `/ohio`, `/utah`, `/medications/oxytocin/michigan`, `/california`, `/oklahoma`, and city-level pages such as `/medications/oxytocin/idaho/twin-falls`. — [Illinois](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin/illinois); [Twin Falls ID](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/medications/oxytocin/idaho/twin-falls)

### Inferences
- **503A-only status is the structural fact that creates the whole market shape.** Because the product must be patient-specific, there can be no office stock, no wholesale list price, and no pharmacy-to-pharmacy commodity competition. Every unit requires a named patient and a prescriber — which is precisely why the telehealth prescribing wrapper (Q2) can charge a multiple of the fill cost.
- **CompoundingFinder is lead generation dressed as price comparison.** "Competing quotes in 1–2 business days" is an RFQ, not a price lookup; its revenue is almost certainly pharmacy referral/lead fees (not stated anywhere I found). That means **real-time oxytocin price transparency does not exist** — a genuine open lane, but also a hard one, because 503A prices are quote-based by regulatory design.
- Its numbers are **internally inconsistent** — "$1.67 to $16.50 per bottle" appears in one snippet alongside "$21 to $120 per fill" in another for the same product. These cannot both be bottle prices; the low figure is probably per-mL or per-dose. **Do not cite the $1.67–$16.50 range as a bottle price.**
- The exhaustive city-level URL lattice is the signature of **programmatic SEO**, which suggests the site's moat is search ranking rather than a real pharmacy network. Its "487 pharmacies"-style authority claims should not be taken at face value.

### Gaps
- **No independent (non-CompoundingFinder) price survey of oxytocin compounding exists** in anything I found. All pharmacy pricing traces to one commercially-motivated source.
- No PCCA, Alliance for Pharmacy Compounding (A4PC) or trade-press commentary on oxytocin specifically surfaced, despite being a suggested source. I could not determine whether oxytocin is on any FDA bulk-substance (503A "Category 1") list or faces any compounding-specific restriction — **an important regulatory unknown for the report.**
- Whether CompoundingFinder charges pharmacies, and how much, is unstated and unverified.
- No 503B outsourcing facility was found making oxytocin nasal spray at all.

## Q4: Does any directory of oxytocin prescribers / "peptide-friendly doctors" exist?

### Takeaway
**No oxytocin-specific directory exists.** But a **peptide-clinic directory layer does already exist and is further along than expected** — chiefly **HealingMaps**, a psychedelic/ketamine directory that has extended into peptides with a claimed 487 verified peptide clinics, and which monetizes not through listing fees alone but through a **group purchasing organization, paid industry intelligence reports, and clinic-side services.** A matching-style site (`mypeptidematch.com`) also exists but could not be characterized. The adjacent-niche comparable, Psychology Today, is an ~$81M/year listings business.

## Q5: How do people currently find an off-label oxytocin prescriber? Path and friction points

### Takeaway
The dominant path is confirmed: **landing page → questionnaire → async telehealth visit → 503A compounding pharmacy → shipped to door**, often inside a company whose main business is something else. The friction points are real but I could only **partially** source them: I could not reach patient-complaint sources (Reddit and forums were blocked/absent from results), so the friction section is weaker than the rest of these notes and leans on provider-side and supply-chain evidence.

### Cited Findings
**The canonical path:**
- "Telehealth platforms have become a viable option for obtaining off-label prescriptions for compounded oxytocin, connecting patients with licensed providers who can conduct virtual consultations to determine eligibility. The typical process involves **booking an appointment, consulting with a licensed healthcare provider to review medical history, receiving a prescription if deemed appropriate, and having the compounded medication shipped directly to your home.**" — [medx.it.com](https://medx.it.com/how-can-i-get-prescribed-oxytocin-a-guide-to-therapeutic-uses-and-pathways) *(SEO site — low confidence, but consistent with seller pages)*
- Heally's version, concretely: "book a free telehealth visit where a licensed provider reviews your needs and writes an oxytocin prescription when appropriate… ships oxytocin nasal spray or oxytocin pills to your door, usually in **24 to 48 hours**… most patients finish the visit in **about 15 minutes**." — [Heally](https://getheally.com/patients/oxytocin-prescription)
- Push Health's version: "**same day Rx consults available**"; patients "connect with a medical provider who can prescribe oxytocin medication when appropriate." — [Push Health](https://www.pushhealth.com/drugs/oxytocin)
- FormBlends' version: strength "set by your provider, who confirms the right strength and dose for you **during your online visit**"; plan "auto-renews at your selected cadence and can be paused or canceled anytime." — [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/products/oxytocin-nasal-rx)
- The pharmacy step is unavoidable: "Since no major pharmaceutical company currently mass-produces a prescription oxytocin nasal spray, this route of administration is primarily available **through compounding pharmacies under a doctor's prescription.**" — [medx.it.com](https://medx.it.com/can-you-still-get-a-prescription-for-oxytocin)
- Conventional-channel prescribers are listed as an alternative: a prescription "can be written by your obstetrician, midwife, primary care provider, or nurse practitioner." — [medx.it.com](https://medx.it.com/how-can-i-get-prescribed-oxytocin-a-guide-to-therapeutic-uses-and-pathways)

**Friction points — what I could source:**
- *Price opacity / no transparent pricing.* The existence and design of CompoundingFinder — "compare price ranges **before requesting a personalized pharmacy quote**," quotes arriving in "1–2 business days" — is itself evidence that **patients cannot see a price up front.** — [CompoundingFinder](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin)
- *Price dispersion is extreme.* Claimed spread of **$10–$355.50** across forms for the same molecule — [CompoundingFinder](https://www.compoundingfinder.com/cheapest/oxytocin) — against telehealth retail of $115–$206/mo (Q2). A patient has no way to know which side of that spread they are on.
- *Dosing confusion is real and acknowledged by sellers.* Strengths differ between major suppliers — Empower documents **50 IU/mL or 100 IU/mL**, Defy lists **50 units/mL**, FormBlends sells **100 IU/mL** — and FormBlends states the strength is chosen by the provider, not the patient. — [Empower](https://www.empowerpharmacy.com/drugs/oxytocin-nasal-spray); [Defy](https://storemigrationtest.defymedical.com/961-emp); [FormBlends](https://formblends.com/products/oxytocin-nasal-rx)
- *Pharmacokinetics make self-assessment hard.* "Following nasal administration, oxytocin nasal spray has been observed to increase blood levels in as quickly as 30 minutes. However, plasma oxytocin levels were found to return to baseline for some people around 90 minutes after administration, with elevated levels seen at 150 minutes in other people." — [Push Health](https://www.pushhealth.com/drugs/oxytocin)
- *Cold chain is a genuine physical constraint for oxytocin as a molecule.* "Oxytocin is sensitive to heat and needs to be stored in refrigerated warehouses, trucks, and health facilities with temperatures **between 2 and 8°C**"; storage "in too-warm conditions causes it to degrade and become significantly less effective." A multi-country study found "significant barriers to maintaining a consistent cold chain" and that "a significant number of samples of oxytocin injection collected from the market contain unacceptable levels of the active ingredient and related substances," concentrated in "high ambient temperature countries." — [Global Health Supply Chain](https://www.ghsupplychain.org/news/oxytocin-quality-article-provides-concrete-parameters-keeping-drug-cold); [PMC6156023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6156023/); [WHO stability data (PDF)](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/sites/default/files/document_files/150%20stability%20data%20Oxytocin%20Oct2018.pdf)
- *Prescriber reluctance is implicit in how sellers hedge.* Push Health's own content concedes use "remains investigational and requires clinician oversight within compounding frameworks such as section 503A"; every seller page repeats the off-label disclaimer. Safety framing cited as the reason for Rx-only status includes "uterine hyperstimulation, cardiovascular issues, uterine rupture, seizures, and water intoxication." — [Push Health](https://www.pushhealth.com/drugs/oxytocin); [medx.it.com](https://medx.it.com/is-oxytocin-a-drug-you-can-buy-understanding-the-prescription-requirements-and-risks)
- *Side-effect profile as marketed:* "Intranasal oxytocin is generally well tolerated, with **headache, transient nasal irritation, and mild nausea** the most common complaints; serious adverse events are rare." — [Push Health](https://www.pushhealth.com/drugs/oxytocin)
- *Community dosing norms are being set by forums, not clinicians.* Peptide Protocol Wiki documents only **3 community protocols from 70 community reports** for oxytocin, used "primarily for social anxiety, social bonding, emotional well-being, and general anxiety reduction," and warns these are "all **off-label uses extrapolated from research trials**" and "not medical advice." — [Peptide Protocol Wiki](https://www.peptideprotocolwiki.com/peptides/oxytocin/community)
- A patient-facing clinical handout for oxytocin nasal spray exists from an academic center, indicating legitimate supervised use — [UW Health Online](https://healthonline.washington.edu/sites/default/files/record_pdfs/09-Oxytocin_Nasal_Spray_8_09.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Discovery today is search-ranking-mediated, not directory-mediated.** Patients type a query and land on whichever seller or SEO site ranks. That is why the programmatic-SEO sites (CompoundingFinder, FormBlends' peptide hub, medx.it.com, medfinder.com) exist at all: **they are monetizing the absence of a directory** by intercepting intent. A real connective layer would be competing against SEO arbitrage, not against nothing.
- **The friction is concentrated at exactly two points a connective layer could address:** (1) *finding a prescriber who will say yes* — currently solved by trial-and-error across platforms; (2) *knowing whether the price is fair* — currently unsolvable because 503A pricing is quote-based. Of these, (1) is the more tractable product.
- **The cold-chain evidence must be used carefully.** All of it concerns **injectable oxytocin in obstetric supply chains in low-resource settings** — it is strong evidence that oxytocin is a thermolabile molecule, but it is **not** evidence about US consumer nasal-spray shipping. I found **no** direct source on US compounded-nasal-spray shipping or refrigeration complaints. The report writer should not imply otherwise.
- The 70-report / 3-protocol figure suggests the **consumer user base is small** — orders of magnitude below GLP-1 or TRT peptide interest. That caps the size of any directory opportunity.

### Gaps
- **Patient complaint evidence is thin and I could not reach the primary sources.** A direct Reddit search returned no Reddit results at all (the index returned seller pages and a suspicious domain, `ftp.pink-ribbon.be`, instead). **I have no first-person patient complaints about price, pharmacy switching, shipping, or prescriber refusal.** The friction list above is inferred from market structure and provider-side documents, not from patient voice. This is the weakest section of these notes.
- **"Pharmacy switching" friction specifically: unverified.** I found no source documenting patients being moved between compounding pharmacies.
- No data on prescription **denial/refusal rates**, consultation **conversion rates**, or subscription **churn** at any platform.
- No source on whether compounded oxytocin nasal spray actually ships cold in the US, or its assigned beyond-use date.

## Cross-cutting summary for the report writer

### Is there a missing connective layer? A calibrated answer
**Partly — but less than the premise assumes, and not where the premise points.**

1. **An oxytocin-specific directory does not exist.** Verified. But the provider set is too small, too unbranded, and too non-specialist (Q1) to support one. "Oxytocin directory" is probably not a viable unit.
2. **A peptide-clinic directory already exists and is credible and funded** — HealingMaps, with ~487 claimed peptide clinics, directory-derived pricing benchmarks, a GPO, paid clinic intelligence reports, and a Dealroom profile. **Any entrant collides with its expansion path.**
3. **A pharmacy price-comparison layer nominally exists but does not really work** — CompoundingFinder is an RFQ lead-gen funnel ("quotes in 1–2 business days"), not price transparency, and 503A regulation makes true live pricing structurally hard.
4. **The genuinely vacant slot is independent vetting.** Every intermediary found is conflicted: a seller running a "find a doctor" directory (FormBlends), a lead broker running "price comparison" (CompoundingFinder), an unsourced "3 vetted options" claim (MedFinder), and AI-generated buying guides on `lovable.app` subdomains. **No neutral quality signal exists anywhere in this market.**
5. **The clearest structural hole is clinical, not informational:** nobody connects *prescribers* (who can write for oxytocin but don't do relational work) to *couples/sex therapists* (who do the work but can't prescribe). The 2013 Glendale clinic is the only example of anyone trying, and it did not become a category.

### Hardest constraints on the opportunity
- **Small user base.** 70 community reports for oxytocin (Q5); grey-market vendors at £30/10 mg with no margin (Q6). This is not a GLP-1-scale market.
- **Weak evidence base vs. aggressive marketing.** Sellers market "pair-bond" and "before shared time"; the literature is social cognition/anxiety/PTSD, with expert skepticism on record since 2013 (Q1). A vetting-based business inherits this credibility problem.
- **Commoditization pressure from above and below** — $10 veterinary vials and $21–$120 pharmacy fills bracket a $115–$206/month retail product.
- **Regulatory tail risk:** an approved intranasal oxytocin product would likely curtail the compounded channel the entire consumer market depends on.
- **Professional-body resistance:** menopause societies explicitly prohibit promotional use of their practitioner directories, signalling how credentialing bodies treat commercialized vetting.

### Overall confidence
- **High:** 503A-only status; the questionnaire→async→compounder path; absence of any oxytocin-specific or oxytocin-therapist directory; absence of "OxytocinBio.com"; existence and approximate pricing of FormBlends / Healthspan / Heally / HealthRX; Psychology Today's $29.95/mo and ~$81M figures; veterinary price bleed; HealingMaps' existence and multi-pronged monetization.
- **Medium:** the $115–$206/mo telehealth band; Empower's 50/100 IU/mL strengths; the claimed $21–$120 nasal-spray fill range; HealingMaps' 487/1,800 counts (self-asserted).
- **Low / unverified:** Superpower's $89; all UK grey-market pricing; `mypeptidematch.com`'s nature; MedFinder's "$25/month" and "3 vetted options"; PepFinder's existence; the ten unverified telehealth brands (Invigor, Defiance Health, Bayview, CostPlusTRT, Kare-Rx, Celia Health, Nutree, Newtropin, AnthologyRX, Maximus); Mochi and TrimRx for oxytocin; any US consumer cold-chain/shipping complaint; current status of Wellness Solutions Glendale.
