## Q2. The replication story and the 2026 consensus on single-dose behavioural effects in healthy adults

### Takeaway
The chain of critiques from 2015 to 2021 (Nave/Camerer/McCullough → Walum/Waldman/Young → Leng & Ludwig → Declerck's high-powered registered replication) demolished the specific "oxytocin increases trust" claim and established that the classic literature was severely underpowered. But the field did **not** settle into a flat null: Quintana's 2021 *Molecular Psychiatry* review argued the nose-to-brain route is real and functionally relevant, and a 2025–2026 follow-up reportedly recovers a *conditional* trust effect in low-trust individuals — i.e. the 2026 consensus appears to be "no reliable main effect of a single dose in unselected healthy adults, with live debate over moderated/subgroup effects."

### Cited Findings
- **Kosfeld et al. 2005** (*Nature*) is the origin claim: intranasal administration of oxytocin increases transfers in the trust game. Described as "an influential 2005 paper showing that intranasal administration of oxytocin increases transfers in the trust game" — [Springer Nature Communities, "Oxytocin and Trust"](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/oxytocin-and-trust)
- **Nave, Camerer & McCullough 2015** (critical review, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*): "the simplest promising finding associating intranasal oxytocin with higher trust has not replicated well" and "the cumulative evidence does not provide robust convergent evidence that human trust is reliably associated with oxytocin (or caused by it)." They reviewed three evidence streams — intranasal administration, plasma-oxytocin correlations, and OXTR polymorphisms — and found the plasma evidence "flawed by how oxytocin is measured in peripheral bodily fluids," with recent large-sample studies failing "to find consistent associations of specific oxytocin-related genetic polymorphisms and trust" — [Nave, Camerer & McCullough 2015, PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~memccullough/Papers/NaveCamererMcCullough15.pdf); [Caltech authors record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/rj5sw-bsb59)
- Contemporaneous reception was blunt: McCullough's own blog post on the review is titled "Human Oxytocin Research Gets a Drubbing" (20 May 2015) — [michael-mccullough.com](https://michael-mccullough.com/social-science-evolving/blog/2015/05/20/human-oxytocin-research-gets-a-drubbing)
- **Walum, Waldman & Young** — "Statistical and methodological considerations for the interpretation of intranasal oxytocin studies," *Biological Psychiatry*. **The average statistical power of human intranasal oxytocin studies is ~16%**, and the authors concluded many published oxytocin effects "might actually reflect Type I errors"; intranasal oxytocin studies are "considerably underpowered and there is a high probability that most of the published findings do not represent true effects" — [PMC4690817](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4690817). **Date conflict to resolve:** the search tool reported the publication year as 2015 in one sentence and cited "Walum et al. (2016)" in the next; the PMC record is PMC4690817. The assignment brief says 2016. Treat the year as 2015/2016 (online 2015, issue 2016 is the most likely reconciliation) and verify before printing.
- A later, independent power analysis reaches the same place: "Most oxytocin administration studies are statistically underpowered to reliably detect (or reject) a wide range of effect sizes" — [PMC9216440](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9216440/)
- **Leng & Ludwig, "Intranasal Oxytocin: Myths and Delusions"** (*Biological Psychiatry*): most intranasally administered oxytocin enters the **peripheral** circulation, and peripheral oxytocin concentration "is not related to central oxytocin activity." They estimate **as little as 0.002% of peripherally administered oxytocin finds its way into the CNS**, and that it has not been shown any of it reaches deep brain areas. Because central receptors are normally exposed to much higher oxytocin levels than peripheral ones, central receptors need comparatively high exposure to produce behavioural effects. They call for proper dose–response studies controlling for peripheral action and for more selective pharmacological agents — [PMC4718566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4718566)
- **Declerck et al. 2020**, *Nature Human Behaviour* (**Registered Report**, doi 10.1038/s41562-020-0878-x): **n = 677 males**, collected in Magdeburg (Otto-von-Guericke University) and Antwerp, with the University of Zurich; **24 IU** intranasal oxytocin vs identical saline placebo. The replication had **>95% statistical power** and **found no effect of oxytocin on trusting behaviour in the main social-contact condition**. Exploratory post-hoc analyses suggested oxytocin "may increase trust in individuals with a low disposition to trust in a no-social-contact condition, but this finding requires confirmation in future research"; the authors stress it "was not anticipated and should be treated with caution" — [Springer Nature Communities](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/oxytocin-and-trust); [U. Antwerp blog](https://blog.uantwerpen.be/business-economics/what-makes-us-trust-another-person-a-behavioural-study-on-the-role-of-oxytocin/); [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v4y2020i6d10.1038_s41562-020-0878-x.html)
- Declerck and colleagues drew an explicitly meta-scientific lesson: "only by preregistering studies with explicitly formulated hypotheses and statistical tests will researchers be able to properly assess the meaning and credibility of results including possible null findings" — [U. Antwerp blog](https://blog.uantwerpen.be/business-economics/what-makes-us-trust-another-person-a-behavioural-study-on-the-role-of-oxytocin/)
- **The 2025–2026 reversal attempt.** A paper/preprint titled **"Oxytocin increases trust in humans with a low disposition to trust"** exists as a bioRxiv preprint posted ~1 October 2025 (10.1101/2025.10.01.679711), and press coverage dated **12 August 2026** is headlined "Oxytocin selectively increases trusting behavior in men with low disposition to trust" / "Oxytocin Selectively Boosts Trust" — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.01.679711.full.pdf); [News-Medical, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260812/Oxytocin-selectively-increases-trusting-behavior-in-men-with-low-disposition-to-trust.aspx); [AlphaGalileo press release](https://www.alphagalileo.org/en-gb/Item-Display/ItemId/276376). **UNVERIFIED:** I could not retrieve the preprint (bioRxiv blocked) and so have **no sample size, no effect size, no journal of record, and no confirmation that the Declerck group are the authors** — though the AlphaGalileo/News-Medical pairing and the hypothesis being exactly Declerck's 2020 post-hoc strongly suggest a confirmatory follow-up by that group. This is the single most important item for the report writer to verify directly.
- **Quintana et al. 2021**, "Advances in the field of intranasal oxytocin research: lessons learned and future directions for clinical research," *Molecular Psychiatry* 26:80–91 — the field's rehabilitation case. It "outlines recent advances from human and animal research that provide converging evidence for functionally relevant effects of the intranasal oxytocin administration route, suggesting that **direct nose-to-brain delivery underlies the behavioral effects** of oxytocin on social cognition and behavior," while addressing "pre-registration, reproducibility, statistical power, interpretation of non-significant results, dosage, and sex differences" and flagging "inconsistent dosing protocols and limited human pharmacokinetic data on nasal-to-CNS transfer" — [PMC7815514](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7815514/)
- Quintana's stated methodological programme: improving "the precision of intranasal oxytocin research … by the complementary consideration of methodology, theory and reproducibility," with "precise sample size estimation, synthetic datasets, and ways to test evidence for null models"; and he notes results from animal models "have failed to reliably translate from animal models to human research," with "calls to improve intranasal oxytocin delivery methodology in terms of verifying that intranasal administration increases central levels of oxytocin" — [Quintana profile, University of Oslo](https://sv.uio.no/psi/english/people/academic/danielqu/index.html)

### Inferences
- The two critiques are **logically independent and both still standing in 2026**: a statistical one (power ~16% ⇒ the pre-2015 literature cannot be trusted) and a pharmacokinetic one (≈0.002% CNS penetration ⇒ a mechanism problem). Quintana's 2021 review contests the second but not the first. So "the field recovered" claims should be read narrowly as a defence of the delivery route, not of the original findings.
- The 2026 trust paper, if it holds, converts a dead headline claim ("oxytocin is the trust molecule") into a live but much weaker and more constrained one (a moderated effect, in men, conditional on low baseline trust *and* absence of social cues). That shape — null main effect, surviving interaction — is the field's dominant 2026 pattern, and it recurs independently in autism (dose moderation), schizophrenia (dose and chronicity moderation) and wound healing (context moderation). For forecasting: the field's modal 2026 position is **not** "oxytocin does nothing," it is "oxytocin does nothing on average," which is a materially different base rate for future subgroup-targeted readouts.
- A conditional effect recovered by post-hoc analysis and then confirmed is the strongest methodological rescue available, but it is also exactly the pattern that produced the original crisis. The report writer should weight the 2026 result by whether it was preregistered — which I could not determine.

### Gaps
- Exact year and volume/pages for Walum et al. (2015 vs 2016 conflict, above).
- Whether any 2024–2026 *editorial* or *consensus statement* explicitly declares the single-dose healthy-adult question closed. I found no such document; the closest is the 2025 special-issue editorial (Q5) which reads as neither triumphal nor abandoning.
- The 2026 trust paper's design, n, power, preregistration and journal — **blocked**.

## Q3. Indication scorecard with latest evidence and dates

### Takeaway
Across the behavioural/psychiatric indications the record is of large, well-powered negative trials (autism NEJM 2021; Prader-Willi Phase 3 2025; alcohol multisite 2026), while the live and growing programme has migrated to **endocrine replacement** (oxytocin deficiency in pituitary disease) and to **peripheral/local** uses (vaginal gel for GSM, wound healing as a context-dependent effect). The clearest single data point in the window is Acadia's September 2025 Phase 3 failure, which killed the most advanced oxytocin-analogue asset and was followed by the sponsor abandoning the molecule outright.

### Cited Findings — Autism (strong negative, with a dose-moderation caveat)
- **Sikich et al. 2021, NEJM.** 24-week trial, ages 3–17, **n = 290** (146 oxytocin / 144 placebo), UNC Chapel Hill. Primary outcome **Aberrant Behavior Checklist modified Social Withdrawal subscale (ABC-mSW)**: least-squares mean change **−3.7 (oxytocin) vs −3.5 (placebo), P = 0.61**. No difference on secondary outcomes generally; similar incidence and severity of adverse events. The accompanying editorial: **"These results do not support the current off-label use of oxytocin in the treatment of autism spectrum disorder."** — [HealthDay/physician coverage](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/conditions/intranasal-oxytocin-therapy-no-benefit-for-children-with-asd-2655289019.html); [Medicom conference report, "Nasal oxytocin fails in large double-blind test"](https://conferences.medicom-publishers.com/specialisation/neurology/nasal-oxytocin-fails-in-large-double-blind-test-as-treatment-for-autism/); [Neuroscience News, "Hormone Widely Used as an Autism Treatment Shows No Benefit"](https://neurosciencenews.com/oxytocin-autism-19469)
- **The 12-RCT / 733-participant meta-analysis** found **no statistically significant effect of intranasal oxytocin on social functioning** in ASD and concluded "the current evidence does not support a significant therapeutic benefit of intranasal oxytocin for improving core behavioral and social symptoms in adults or children with ASD," while cautioning that small samples and heterogeneous designs "warrant caution when interpreting the clinical generalizability of these null findings." The matching record I located is an **AAN 2026 abstract**, "Efficacy of Chronic Intranasal Oxytocin Administration in Individuals with Autism: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis" — [AAN 2026 abstract 004551](https://index.mirasmart.com/AAN2026/PDFfiles/AAN2026-004551.html). **Partially UNVERIFIED:** the search tool returned the 12-RCT/733 figures and the quoted conclusions in response to a query naming them, but I could not confirm the n=733 belongs specifically to the AAN 2026 abstract rather than to a separate 2026 journal meta-analysis. Verify before citing the pairing.
- A **distinct** dose–response meta-analysis — "Optimal dose of oxytocin to improve social impairments and repetitive behaviors in autism spectrum disorders," **12 RCTs / 498 ASD patients** — found **no significant effect on social impairments overall**, but "for a high dose of **48 IU per day**, a beneficial effect on social impairments was found" — [PMC11813649](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11813649/); published version at [Altern Ther / IMR Press AP 27(4) 45322](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/AP/27/4/10.31083/AP45322)
- **Note the collision:** two meta-analyses both report "12 RCTs" with different participant totals (733 vs 498) and opposite-signed subgroup conclusions. The report writer must not merge them.

### Cited Findings — Schizophrenia (negative overall; dose and chronicity moderation)
- Systematic review, meta-analysis and dose–response meta-analysis of RCTs: across **all 9 identified RCTs**, intranasal oxytocin showed **no significant effect on negative symptoms**; but at **higher doses (>40–80 IU)** a beneficial effect on negative symptoms was found "with a moderate effect size," and the dose–response model "predicted that higher doses of oxytocin may be more efficacious for negative symptoms" — [PMC8378078](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8378078/)
- A second meta-analysis: oxytocin "was not different from placebo for negative symptoms overall," but a **sensitivity analysis restricted to consecutive-day (chronic) dosing** found oxytocin superior to placebo on negative symptoms — [PMC8378078 search context](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8378078/); related Fujita Health University record: ["Efficacy and safety of oxytocin augmentation therapy for schizophrenia"](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/efficacy-and-safety-of-oxytocin-augmentation-therapy-for-schizoph/)
- The field's own framing is explicit that the problem is unsolved: a review titled "Oxytocin effects in schizophrenia: Reconciling mixed findings and moving forward," and context noting that "currently available pharmacological agents have proven minimally efficacious for remediating negative symptoms, making oxytocin a potentially valuable area of investigation despite the inconsistent findings" — [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/151391327)
- Still-active development: a study protocol for **oxytocin-augmented group psychotherapy for negative symptoms in schizophrenia-spectrum disorders**, *European Psychiatry* — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/oxytocinaugmented-group-psychotherapy-for-negative-symptoms-in-patients-with-schizophrenia-spectrum-disorders-a-study-protocol/177B40D2CDFC984F2589A2FF07E5E357). **UNVERIFIED:** no n, site or completion date obtained.

### Cited Findings — Wound healing (the notable 2025/2026 positive, but context-dependent)
- **Schneider E, Hernández C, Brock R, et al. ### Inferences
- **The centre of gravity has moved out of psychiatry and into endocrinology.** Every well-powered psychiatric/behavioural readout in the window is negative (autism 2021, PWS 2025, and probably AUD 2026), while the trials that are *starting* and *expanding* — Basel's OxyTUTION/OxyPLEASURE, Lawson's adult and newly-registered paediatric deficiency studies (NCT07568509 is a 2025/2026-vintage number) — are about diagnosing and replacing a genuine hormone deficiency in a defined patient population. That is a fundamentally different and more favourable scientific setup: a measurable deficiency state, an objective provocation test, and a replacement rationale, rather than an enhancement rationale in people with normal oxytocin.
- **The three indications with positive signals in 2025–2026 all avoid the thing that failed.** Vaginal gel for GSM is local delivery to tissue with abundant oxytocin receptors (no blood–brain barrier problem). Wound healing worked only as a *potentiator* of behaviour, not as a drug. Oxytocin deficiency is replacement, not augmentation. This is a coherent pattern and the most defensible organising claim available for the report: **oxytocin is surviving where it is used as a hormone and dying where it was used as a psychoactive.**
- Acadia's September 2025 decision ("does not intend to investigate intranasal carbetocin any further") is a strong negative signal for large-pharma appetite. TNX-2900 entering Phase 2 in 2026 does not offset it: Tonix is a small-cap, the asset's differentiation is a formulation tweak (magnesium potentiation) rather than a new mechanism, and the economic driver includes a transferable Priority Review Voucher, which is a partially non-efficacy incentive.
- COMPASS PWS's failure on **every** secondary endpoint, not just the primary, is more damaging than a near-miss would have been, and it was a 175-patient trial — i.e. not a power problem. Combined with Sikich 2021 (n=290, P=0.61), the field now has two adequately-powered, unambiguous negatives in paediatric social/appetitive indications.

## Q4. What explains the heterogeneity; 2025–2026 responder phenotypes and biomarkers

### Takeaway
The field's own candidate explanations are dose (with converging dose–response signals at 40–48 IU and above), chronic vs single dosing, social context at the time of administration, and individual moderators (OXTR genotype, baseline trait, sex). The most concrete 2025–2026 rescue attempt is **neurophysin-I as a stable surrogate assay for oxytocin secretion**, which reportedly separates oxytocin-deficient patients from controls with ROC-AUC 100% under MDMA provocation — a diagnostic, not a treatment-response, biomarker.

### Cited Findings — Dose
- Autism: no overall effect, but **48 IU/day** showed benefit on social impairments in a dose–response meta-analysis of 12 RCTs / 498 patients — [PMC11813649](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11813649/)
- Schizophrenia: no overall effect across 9 RCTs, but **>40–80 IU** gave a moderate-effect-size benefit on negative symptoms, and the dose–response model predicted higher doses would be more efficacious — [PMC8378078](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8378078/)
- Mechanistic basis for a high-dose requirement: central oxytocin receptors are normally exposed to far higher concentrations than peripheral ones, so "oxytocin receptors in the brain need to be exposed to comparatively high amounts of oxytocin to produce behavioral effects — sometimes as much as 5 to 100 nanograms," against an estimated **0.002%** CNS penetration — [Leng & Ludwig, PMC4718566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4718566)
- NB: the two meta-analyses converge on roughly the same threshold (≳40 IU/day) from independent indications. That is the single most quantitatively specific heterogeneity finding in this report.

### Cited Findings — Chronic vs single dosing
- Schizophrenia: a sensitivity analysis **restricted to consecutive-day dosing** found oxytocin superior to placebo on negative symptoms, where the pooled analysis did not — [PMC8378078](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8378078/)
- Counterpoint: the two large chronic-dosing trials that followed were negative — Sikich 2021 dosed over **24 weeks** (P = 0.61) and COMPASS PWS dosed three times daily to **Week 12** (no separation on primary or any secondary) — [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/conditions/intranasal-oxytocin-therapy-no-benefit-for-children-with-asd-2655289019.html); [Acadia](https://acadia.com/en-us/media/news-releases/acadia-pharmaceuticals-announces-phase-3-compass-pws-trial)

### Cited Findings — Social context and formulation/device
- Context dependence is now demonstrated experimentally in humans: in the JAMA Psychiatry wound-healing RCT, **oxytocin had no effect alone**; it worked only in combination with affectionate behaviour, which the authors read as oxytocin "transfer[ring] the positive effects of closeness and good relationship experiences to the body" — [idw](https://nachrichten.idw-online.de/2025/12/16/when-partnership-gets-under-your-skin-how-oxytocin-and-tenderness-promote-wound-healing)
- Context dependence in animals too: intranasal oxytocin reduced alcohol intake in **single-housed** mice but was ineffective in **group-housed** mice — the authors frame this as oxytocin losing effectiveness "under translationally relevant conditions like intranasal administration and social settings, which could reflect its limited success in clinical trials" — [Psychopharmacology s00213-025-06962-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-025-06962-0)
- Declerck 2020's own conditional result was a **context** interaction: the suggestive effect appeared in the **no-social-contact** condition, not the social-contact condition — [Springer Nature Communities](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/oxytocin-and-trust)
- Formulation as an explicit development hypothesis: TNX-2900's magnesium potentiation is designed "to improve receptor binding and **decrease dose-related inconsistencies in receptor activity**" — i.e. a sponsor betting money that pharmacological inconsistency, not absent biology, caused prior failures — [Tonix TNX-2900](https://www.tonixpharma.com/tnx-2900/)
- Quintana's 2021 review explicitly names **inconsistent dosing protocols and limited human pharmacokinetic data on nasal-to-CNS transfer** as unresolved, alongside **sex differences** — [PMC7815514](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7815514/)

### Cited Findings — Genetics, individual moderators, and the precision-medicine turn
- Nave/Camerer/McCullough's negative verdict covers OXTR: "in recent large-sample studies, researchers failed to find consistent associations of specific oxytocin-related genetic polymorphisms and trust" — [Nave et al. 2015](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~memccullough/Papers/NaveCamererMcCullough15.pdf)
- Nonetheless the field's 2025 framing retains OXTR as a moderator: oxytocin effects on social behaviour and brain function "vary from one individual to another based on factors such as heterogeneity in autism phenotype, childhood experiences, personality, attachment style, and **oxytocin receptor polymorphisms**," with the conclusion that "targeted therapies for subgroups of patients can help alleviate core symptoms" — [Editorial, "Current Advances in Oxytocin Research," *Curr Issues Mol Biol*, May 2025, PMC12110669](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12110669/)
- The precision-medicine programme is explicit and aligned to NIMH RDoC: the need to understand "how individual social aptitudes, emotional regulation, receptor distribution, and genetic polymorphisms affect the social outcome of oxytocin-based treatments," in a "precision medicine" approach aligned with RDoC — [editorial/review context, PMC12110669](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12110669/); see also ["A precision medicine approach to oxytocin trials"](https://read.qxmd.com/read/28812273/a-precision-medicine-approach-to-oxytocin-trials)
- The field names its own open question in exactly the terms the report needs: "Key outstanding questions include **whether biomarkers of oxytocin system function can reliably predict who will respond to intranasal oxytocin treatment**," against the backdrop that "hundreds of studies [have been] conducted but **no oxytocin-based treatment for social behavior approved**" — [PMC12110669 context](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12110669/)
- Baseline-trait moderation is the live 2026 claim: the 2025/2026 trust result is framed around "men with low disposition to trust" — [News-Medical, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260812/Oxytocin-selectively-increases-trusting-behavior-in-men-with-low-disposition-to-trust.aspx)

### Cited Findings — Neurophysin-I (the leading 2025–2026 biomarker candidate)
- The measurement problem it solves: "diagnosis and clinical progress have been hindered by challenges in reliably measuring oxytocin due to its **instability and short half-life**" — [PMC12668196](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12668196/)
- **Neurophysin I (NP-I)** is "an equimolarly co-released cleavage product of the OXT precursor peptide" and "offers a promising alternative biomarker due to its stability and longer half-life"; it "is secreted in equimolar concentrations with oxytocin but has a longer half-life, circulates in higher concentrations, and can be measured using a **sandwich immunoassay**" — [PMC12668196](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12668196/)
- **Diagnostic performance:** "MDMA-stimulated NP-1 demonstrated strong diagnostic performance in differentiating patients with OXT deficiency from healthy controls with a **ROC-AUC of 100%**," presented at the **2025 Endocrine Society conference (ENDO 2025)** — [Endocrine Abstracts ea0110p861](https://www.endocrine-abstracts.org/ea/0110/ea0110p861); [PMC12668196](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12668196/)
- Independent validation line: "Neurophysin I is an analytically robust surrogate biomarker for oxytocin" (Arizona; MacLean group) — [University of Arizona Experts record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/neurophysin-i-is-an-analytically-robust-surrogate-biomarker-for-o/); [UA Anthropology news](https://anthropology.arizona.edu/news/neurophysin-i-analytically-robust-surrogate-biomarker-oxytocin); [UA repository](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/670761); [MacLean 2024 PDF](https://dogs.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2026-06/MacLean_2024.pdf)
- Framing of the deficiency entity itself: "Oxytocin (OXT) deficiency is a **recently identified new neuroendocrine entity** associated with anxiety and reduced prosocial behavior" — [PMC12668196](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12668196/)

### Inferences
- **Neurophysin-I is a diagnostic rescue, not a treatment-response rescue, and the distinction matters for forecasting.** A ROC-AUC of 100% separating deficient patients from controls validates *who is deficient*; it says nothing about *who responds to replacement*. The field's own stated open question (does any biomarker predict response?) remains unanswered as of the 2025 editorial. Forecasts about "biomarker-stratified oxytocin trials succeeding" should therefore be conditioned on a step that has not yet been taken.
- A ROC-AUC of exactly 100% in a conference abstract is a small-sample red flag, not a mature validation. It will almost certainly fall on replication in larger and more heterogeneous cohorts. Treat it as promising-but-fragile.
- **The dose hypothesis is the most falsifiable surviving explanation, and it is about to be tested cleanly.** Two independent meta-analyses point to ≳40 IU/day; NCT04903002 compares 24 IU vs 48 IU vs placebo within-subject in 336 patients, with primary completion 31 March 2026. If 48 IU separates from 24 IU and placebo there, the dose-moderation story gains real support; if neither dose beats placebo, the most popular remaining excuse for the null literature weakens considerably.
- There is an unresolved tension the report should name rather than smooth over: context-dependence is offered both as an *explanation* for past failures (effects need the right social setting) and as a *finding* (wound healing). But an agent that only works when paired with an effective behavioural intervention is, regulatorily and commercially, a much weaker product than a drug — and it is also harder to distinguish from a moderator-fishing artefact.

### Gaps
- Sex differences: named by Quintana 2021 as a methodological issue, but I retrieved **no quantitative sex-difference estimate**. Note that Declerck 2020 (n=677) and the 2026 follow-up were **both male-only**, which limits generalisation in a direction the report should state.
- No 2025–2026 paper proposing a *treatment-response* responder phenotype with validated predictive accuracy was found.
- Device/formulation comparisons (standard spray vs breath-powered/Optinose-type delivery) — no findings.

- **The single result that would most change the field's direction is a positive OxyTUTION** (or the equivalent in the Basel/MGH deficiency programme). A positive result would establish oxytocin deficiency as a treatable endocrine disorder — a first-in-class indication with a diagnostic test (MDMA/neurophysin-I provocation) already in hand — and would reframe three decades of behavioural nulls as having targeted the wrong population. A negative result, conversely, would close off the field's one remaining high-prior pathway, since every augmentation indication has already failed at adequate power. Its n=112 is modest, so a negative should be read cautiously, but a positive would be highly informative.
- Second-most consequential: **NCT04903002**, because it is the cleanest available test of the dose hypothesis (within-subject, two doses, n=336) in a non-social indication where the Leng/Ludwig peripheral-action critique is an *advantage* rather than a problem — peripheral oxytocin receptors are plausibly relevant to nociception, so a positive here does not require nose-to-brain transport to be true.
- **TNX-2900's Phase 2 is the field's cheapest falsification test of the "formulation, not biology" excuse.** It targets the same indication and broadly the same population that just failed in a 175-patient Phase 3 with a long-acting oxytocin analogue. If a magnesium-potentiated formulation succeeds where carbetocin failed, formulation genuinely mattered; if it fails, the PWS hypothesis is finished and so is the formulation defence.
- Given that the only post-2026 items I can document are "initiation planned 2026," the honest characterisation for the forecast is that **the oxytocin pipeline has no visible, dated Phase 3 readouts between 2027 and 2030.** Any probability mass the report assigns to an approval in that window should rest on trials not yet registered or not visible in my searches, and should be low for that reason.

- Primary endpoints for NCT06514586, NCT06460948, NCT07568509, OxyPLEASURE, OxyTUTION (exact instrument) and the TNX-2900 Phase 2 — not retrieved.
- Estimated completion dates for the GSM Phase 3 and the MGH deficiency studies — not retrieved.

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The researchers who are still publicly bullish have **changed the claim**, not defended the old one. Quintana defends the delivery route and prescribes better methods; Lawson and Christ-Crain have moved to a deficiency-replacement frame; Heidelberg reframes oxytocin as a potentiator of behaviour. Nobody in the sources I found defends "oxytocin improves social behaviour in unselected people."
- That consensus shape is itself a forecastable quantity: the probability of a *new, large, single-dose behavioural trial in healthy adults* producing a positive headline result should be set low, because the field has largely stopped running them, and the one recent instance recovered only a conditional effect in a pre-identified subgroup.
