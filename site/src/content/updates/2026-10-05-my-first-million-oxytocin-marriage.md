## What happened

On 5 October 2026, the business podcast My First Million released episode 868. The show notes list "Oxytocin dosing" as the first segment, starting at 3:08, followed by "Loneliness solutions" and "Faith-based businesses" [1][2]. A same-day aggregator post carried the episode under the headline "The next Ozempic might fix marriages" [3].

Hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr framed oxytocin dosing as a business idea. The idea traces to Palmer Luckey, the founder of Oculus and Anduril, who floated "oxytocin doping for marriage counseling" on the same podcast in October 2022 (episode 378), noting that the hormone is cheap and, in his words, barely regulated. The 2026 episode revived it. The pitch, as it traveled this time, was a telehealth-style compounded oxytocin product for couples, modeled on the GLP-1 weight-loss boom.

That idea is not new. Press stories about a "nasal spray that might save a marriage" ran in 2011 to 2013, and experts were already warning then against using oxytocin in couples therapy [9].

## What the evidence actually says

The strongest hint of a couples effect is a 2009 Swiss lab study. Forty-seven couples argued about a real conflict after sniffing oxytocin or placebo. The oxytocin couples showed more positive communication relative to negative, and lower cortisol afterward [4]. One session, one argument, in a lab. It has not been shown to change a relationship.

When oxytocin was tested as an add-on to actual couples therapy, it did nothing. A 2025 trial gave 96 couples either oxytocin or placebo before Alcohol Behavioral Couple Therapy sessions. Oxytocin was well tolerated and added no improvement in drinking or in relationship outcomes beyond therapy alone [5].

A JAMA Psychiatry trial published in 2026 is the one people will cite. In 80 healthy couples with small suction-blister wounds, oxytocin by itself did not speed healing or lower cortisol. Oxytocin combined with affectionate touch was linked to faster healing [6]. Read carefully, that is a study about touch, with oxytocin as a possible amplifier. It is not a study about saving marriages.

The broader "trust molecule" story the pitch leans on failed its decisive test in 2020, when 677 men in a pre-registered replication showed no effect of oxytocin on trust [7].

One trial could change this picture. The VA is running a Phase 2 study in about 120 veteran couples where the veteran takes oxytocin before each of eight couples-therapy sessions for PTSD. Completion is expected around 2028 [8].
