## What happened

On 27 May 2026, Biohaven held its annual R&D Day. Among the presentations was BHV-1955, which the company describes as "a novel, long-acting intranasal formulation of oxytocin, for the central treatment of tinnitus" [1][2]. The presenter was Lawrence C. Newman, the neurologist who led the only registered oxytocin trial for tinnitus in the United States [1].

Biohaven acquired rights to oxytocin derivatives for tinnitus through an agreement with a company called Maskbegone, with an upfront payment in the first quarter of 2026 [2]. BHV-1955 sits in the preclinical part of Biohaven's pipeline. The company's first-quarter 2026 results, released shortly before R&D Day, listed no clinical milestone for it [3].

## The NYU history

Newman's involvement ties BHV-1955 to a trial that never finished. In January 2020, NYU Langone started NCT04210310, "High Dose Oxytocin Nasal Spray for Treatment of Tinnitus," a placebo-controlled pilot [4]. Press coverage at the time described 30 patients using oxytocin or placebo spray four times a day for six months [5].

The trial was terminated. The registry gives the reason as the principal investigator's departure from the institution. It reached primary completion in May 2022 and results were submitted in August 2022, but a pilot cut short by a departure is not evidence for or against the drug [4].

Newman is also an inventor, with a colleague named Cramer, on US Patent 11,241,477, "Oxytocin compositions for treatment of tinnitus." The patent was assigned to NYU and later to Maskbegone LLC [7], the same entity Biohaven dealt with. In other words, the NYU idea left with its inventor, was packaged as intellectual property, and has now been licensed to a public company.

## What the evidence actually is

One study. In 2017, a team at the Federal University of São Paulo gave nasal oxytocin to 15 people with tinnitus for 10 weeks in an open-label study, then ran a placebo-controlled crossover in 16 more. Both showed significant reductions in tinnitus scores, and the authors called for larger controlled trials [6]. Nine years later, nobody has published one.

The "long-acting" label is the one genuinely new element. Plain oxytocin lasts about half an hour in the body, which is why the NYU protocol needed four sprays a day. If Biohaven's formulation holds exposure steady, it could make a fair test of the idea possible for the first time.

## What it means

BHV-1955 is a preclinical program with a single 31-person pilot behind it. Biohaven has not announced an IND filing or a Phase 1 start. Our estimate, not the company's, is first human dosing in 2027 at the earliest and no efficacy data before 2028.

Tinnitus patients have been following this thread for years. A long-running forum thread about oxytocin and tinnitus includes people who obtained compounded spray and used the NYU-style schedule for a month with little or no effect, and one who reported it made his tinnitus worse. None of that is data, but it is a reminder that a plausible mechanism is not a treatment.
