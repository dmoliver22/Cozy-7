## The short answer

As of October 2026, no oxytocin-based drug is close to approval for anything beyond labor and postpartum bleeding. Two small US companies are the whole commercial field: Biohaven, with a preclinical long-acting nasal oxytocin for tinnitus, and Tonix, with a magnesium-boosted nasal oxytocin for Prader-Willi syndrome and several academic side studies. The one large-pharma program, Acadia's carbetocin for Prader-Willi, failed its Phase 3 in September 2025 and was shut down.

Outside the brain, a Phase 3 trial of an oxytocin vaginal gel for menopause symptoms is enrolling, and Ferring's lactation drug merotocin, long listed as Phase II in drug databases, had its trial terminated in January 2023 after enrolling four participants.

Here is every program, what it rests on, and when you will hear more.

## The pipeline at a glance

| Program | Company | Indication | Route | Stage (Oct 2026) | Next catalyst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BHV-1955, long-acting oxytocin | Biohaven | Tinnitus | Nasal | Preclinical [1][2] | IND filing or first trial registration; no date announced |
| TNX-2900, magnesium-potentiated oxytocin | Tonix | Prader-Willi hyperphagia | Nasal | Phase 2 now guided for the second half of 2027, ages 8 to 17.5 [3][4] | Trial registration and first patient dosed, late 2027 |
| TNX-1900, same formulation, higher dose | Tonix | AVP deficiency (FOCUS pilot); investigator-led studies in obesity, binge eating, bone health in autism, social anxiety, migraine | Nasal | Academic pilots; FOCUS dosing since Oct 2025 [5][6] | FOCUS results; no date given |
| ACP-101, intranasal carbetocin | Acadia | Prader-Willi hyperphagia | Nasal | Discontinued after Phase 3 failure, Sept 2025 [7] | None |
| Merotocin (FE 202767) | Ferring | Lactation support, mothers of preterm infants | Nasal | Phase II trial terminated January 2023 after four participants; program appears inactive [8] | Phase II readout; no date found |
| Oxytocin vaginal gel | Oxagon (registry sponsor) | Vulvovaginal atrophy after menopause | Topical | Phase 3, 242 planned participants [9] | Trial completion; date not posted |

Compounded oxytocin nasal sprays sold through telehealth are not in this table. They are not drug development programs. They are the same old molecule, made to order by compounding pharmacies, with no trials behind the uses they are marketed for.

## BHV-1955: Biohaven's long-acting nasal oxytocin for tinnitus

### What it is

Biohaven describes BHV-1955 in its 2026 SEC filings as "a novel, long-acting intranasal formulation of oxytocin, for the central treatment of tinnitus" [1]. The company acquired rights to oxytocin derivatives for tinnitus through an agreement with a firm called Maskbegone, with an upfront payment in the first quarter of 2026 [1]. The program sits in the preclinical section of Biohaven's pipeline [1][2].

### Why the company thinks it will work

The idea traces back to one neurologist. Lawrence Newman ran a trial at NYU Langone from 2020 to 2022 that gave people with tinnitus a high-dose oxytocin spray four times a day [10]. He is also an inventor on US Patent 11,241,477, "Oxytocin compositions for treatment of tinnitus," which was assigned to NYU and later to Maskbegone LLC [12]. Newman presented BHV-1955 at Biohaven's R&D Day on 27 May 2026 [2].

The theory is that tinnitus is partly a brain problem, not just an ear problem, and that oxytocin receptors in the auditory pathways could quiet the overactive circuits that produce phantom sound. "Long-acting" is the engineering pitch: plain oxytocin clears in about half an hour, which is why the NYU protocol needed four sprays a day. A formulation that lasts longer could give steadier exposure.

### Evidence so far

Thin. The only published human data is a 2017 Brazilian pilot: 15 patients took nasal oxytocin for 10 weeks in an open-label study, and 16 more took part in a placebo-controlled crossover. Both reported less tinnitus, and the authors called for larger trials [11]. Nobody ran them.

The NYU trial (NCT04210310) was terminated because the investigator left the institution. It completed in May 2022 with results submitted in August 2022, but it was a small pilot cut short and should not be read as evidence either way [10]. BHV-1955 itself has not been tested in people.

### Next catalyst

An IND filing (FDA permission to start human testing) or a first ClinicalTrials.gov registration. Biohaven has announced neither, and its 2026 quarterly materials list no clinical milestone for the program [1]. Our estimate, not the company's: Phase 1 in 2027 at the earliest, first efficacy data no sooner than 2028.

### What success or failure would mean

A positive Phase 2 would be the first controlled evidence that oxytocin helps tinnitus, and it would hit a condition with tens of millions of sufferers and almost no drug options. A failure would close the door that the 2017 pilot opened. Either way, nothing changes for patients for years. See [oxytocin for tinnitus](/oxytocin-for/tinnitus/) for what helps today.

## TNX-2900: Tonix's magnesium-potentiated oxytocin for Prader-Willi syndrome

### What it is

TNX-2900 is a nasal spray combining oxytocin with magnesium. Tonix plans a Phase 2 trial in children and adolescents aged 8 to 17.5 with Prader-Willi syndrome, randomized to one of three doses or placebo for 12 weeks [3][4]. The trial was expected in 2026 and is now planned for the first quarter of 2027 [3]. It holds FDA Orphan Drug and Rare Pediatric Disease designations, which can earn a priority review voucher if the drug is ever approved [4].

### Why the company thinks it will work

Prader-Willi syndrome causes relentless hunger (hyperphagia), and people with the condition have about 42 percent fewer oxytocin-producing neurons in the hypothalamus [14]. Replacing the missing signal is a reasonable idea. Tonix's twist is the magnesium. In animal models, magnesium ions help oxytocin bind its receptor and reduce what Tonix calls "paradoxical high-dose inhibition," where more drug produces less effect [13]. The company argues this gives a more consistent response and lets it use lower doses than past trials.

### Evidence so far

None in Prader-Willi for this formulation. The relevant evidence is mostly bad news for the class: several small academic oxytocin trials in Prader-Willi gave mixed results, and Acadia's carbetocin, a related molecule, failed outright in 175 patients (below). Tonix's human data on the magnesium formulation comes from the FOCUS pilot in adults with vasopressin deficiency, which began dosing in October 2025 and compares single 6 IU and 24 IU doses [5], and from investigator-led studies that have not reported.

### Next catalyst

Registration of the Phase 2 trial on ClinicalTrials.gov and first patient dosed. Tonix first guided to 2026, then to the first quarter of 2027, and its most recent guidance points to the second half of 2027 [3]. Tonix has not stated why the start keeps slipping. With a 12-week treatment period and a rare-disease enrollment pace, topline data are unlikely before late 2028.

### What success or failure would mean

Success would be a first for oxytocin outside obstetrics and would validate the low-dose, potentiated approach that Tonix is also testing in pain, obesity and anxiety. Failure after carbetocin would strongly suggest that nasal oxytocin-receptor drugs do not reach or do not fix the hunger circuits in Prader-Willi, and would likely end commercial interest in the indication. See [oxytocin for weight loss](/oxytocin-for/weight-loss/).

## TNX-1900: the same spray at higher dose, in academic hands

TNX-1900 is the higher-dose version of Tonix's magnesium formulation. Tonix is not running company trials of it. Instead it supplies the drug to investigator-initiated studies. According to Tonix, these include a Phase 1 pharmacodynamic study for migraine and craniofacial pain [6], Phase 2 studies at Massachusetts General Hospital in adolescent obesity, binge eating disorder and bone health in autism, and a study at the University of Washington in social anxiety disorder [6]. The FOCUS pilot in vasopressin deficiency uses both TNX-1900 and TNX-2900 [5].

We list these statuses as Tonix reports them. None has published results, several have no posted completion dates, and we have not independently confirmed that every study is still active. The pain rationale is specific: oxytocin inhibits release of CGRP, the migraine pain signal, in trigeminal nerve pathways, and magnesium made the analgesic effect dose-dependent in animal head-pain models [6][13].

## Intranasal carbetocin (ACP-101): the cautionary tale

Carbetocin is a longer-lasting cousin of oxytocin, approved in many countries by injection to prevent bleeding after cesarean delivery. Acadia Pharmaceuticals developed a nasal version for Prader-Willi hunger and ran COMPASS PWS, a Phase 3 trial in 175 participants aged 5 to 30 over 12 weeks [7].

On 24 September 2025, Acadia announced that the trial missed its primary endpoint, change in the Hyperphagia Questionnaire for Clinical Trials at week 12, and showed no separation from placebo on any secondary endpoint. Safety was consistent with earlier trials. The company discontinued the program [7].

This was the best-funded, best-designed test of an oxytocin-receptor drug for a brain indication ever run. It failed cleanly. Anyone evaluating TNX-2900, or any other nasal oxytocin for appetite or behavior, should start here.

## Merotocin: Ferring's lactation drug

Merotocin (FE 202767) is a selective, short-acting peptide that activates the oxytocin receptor without touching the related vasopressin receptors. Ferring has studied it as a nasal spray to help mothers of premature infants who struggle to produce milk, mirroring the old use of Syntocinon nasal spray for letdown. Its registered Phase II trial is NCT02545127 [8].

The most recent published work is a 2024 study in Breastfeeding Medicine showing that merotocin was undetectable in breast milk after intravenous doses of up to 20 micrograms in postpartum women [15], a safety question that matters for a drug given to nursing mothers.

Drug databases still carry merotocin as Phase II, but the registered trial was terminated in January 2023 after enrolling only four participants, and we have found no company statement since. Treat the program as dormant unless Ferring says otherwise.

## Oxytocin vaginal gel: Phase 3 for menopause

This is the only oxytocin program in the pipeline that does not go up the nose. Vaginal oxytocin gel is being tested for vulvovaginal atrophy, the thinning and dryness that follows menopause, as an alternative for women who cannot or do not want to use estrogen.

The registered Phase 3 trial, NCT06514586, is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of 242 postmenopausal women applying the gel or placebo once daily for 60 days with a 15-day follow-up. The registry lists Oxagon as sponsor [9]. Earlier small trials, pooled in a 2023 meta-analysis, found that clinically assessed atrophy improved but that painful intercourse and vaginal acidity did not differ significantly from placebo. The Phase 3 is sized to settle that.

The catalyst is trial completion and publication; the registry does not yet give a firm date. A positive result would make oxytocin gel a serious non-estrogen option. A negative one would match the pattern elsewhere: a real hormone, a plausible mechanism, and no clinical benefit when properly tested. Our [menopause page](/oxytocin-for/menopause/) covers the gel studies and the Basel work on whether oxytocin falls after menopause.

## What to watch, in order

1. Second half of 2027: Tonix registers and starts the TNX-2900 Phase 2 in Prader-Willi, or delays a third time.
2. Any time: Biohaven files an IND or registers a first trial of BHV-1955.
3. 2027: results from the FOCUS pilot at Massachusetts General Hospital.
4. Unknown: completion of the oxytocin gel Phase 3, and whether Ferring ever revives merotocin.
